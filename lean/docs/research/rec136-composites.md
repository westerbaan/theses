# rec136-composites: can a "no composites for exceptional Jordan algebras" theorem replace Shultz in REC 135/136?

Status: literature survey plus analysis, 2026-09-26. Every result below is **claimed**: no Lean was
written and no adversarial review has been done. Sources read: BGW (arXiv 1606.09331 v-latest, full
text), Barnum–Wilce (arXiv 1202.4513), van de Wetering (arXiv 1803.11139, 1801.05798), Wilce (arXiv
1206.2897), short.tex §6, Monoidal.lean, Rec136Hyps{,2}.lean, rec-citations.md. Hanche-Olsen 1985
(LNM 1132, 223–229, doi 10.1007/BFb0074886) is paywalled: I saw only the statement as quoted by
BW 2014 and vdW 2019. **The proof below is not checked against the source.**

## 1. The candidates

**(a) Hanche-Olsen 1985, "JB-algebras with tensor products are C\*-algebras".** Quoted in BW 2014, §1.4:
> Let E be any JB-algebra, E₂ = M₂(ℂ)_sa, and suppose the *vector space* E ⊗ E₂ carries a
> [JB-]Jordan product with (a⊗1)•(b⊗v) = (a•b)⊗v and (1⊗v)•(a⊗w) = a⊗(v•w). Then E = 𝔄_sa for
> a C\*-algebra 𝔄.

The Jordan hypotheses are exactly REC 125/127 (the fundamental identity, with a ↦ a⊗1 a Jordan hom).
There are two hypotheses REC does not have:
- **Local tomography.** The composite *is* the algebraic tensor product E ⊗ E₂. In REC, V_{A⊗B}
  only contains the span of the a⊗b. The injectivity of the product-state map (REC 127) gives
  E ⊗ E₂ ↪ V_{A⊗B}, but not onto. The JC version (Hanche-Olsen, CJM 35 (1983), Thm 5.5; BGW
  Prop 3.10) adds operator commutation as hypothesis (iii), but it is still E ⊗ M₂(ℂ)_sa as a
  vector space.
- **A qubit factor.** One factor must be M₂(ℂ)_sa. REC assumes no such object: the paper notes
  (short.tex, after Thm `thm:JW-algebra`) that C may be entirely classical. REC's argument
  tensors the exceptional corner with itself.

Its conclusion (C\*-algebra, i.e. complex) is also much stronger than JW. **Not applicable to REC
135.** It is not the content of REC 135 despite the title. Proof idea (not seen; my reconstruction
from the Pauli basis): local tomography forces (a⊗σ_x)•(b⊗σ_y) ∈ E⊗σ_z, which defines a skew
bracket on E that makes E + iE a C\*-algebra. It is inherently locally tomographic and qubit-based.

**(b) Barnum–Graydon–Wilce, Quantum 4, 359 (2020), arXiv 1606.09331.** Finite-dimensional only
(EJAs; the proofs use one-parameter groups on finite-dimensional inner-product spaces, Jordan frames,
rank, and the Jordan–von Neumann–Wigner classification).
- Def 4.1, a composite AB: a bilinear π : A×B → AB with
  - (2.1a) positivity of a⊗b;
  - (2.1b) u⊗u = u;
  - (2.1c) product states exist;
  - (2.3) a homomorphism G(A)×G(B) → G(AB) of the order-automorphism groups;
  - (4.1b) (φ⊗ψ)† = φ†⊗ψ† for the self-dualising inner product;
  - (4.1c) AB is generated by pure tensors.
- Derived: Prop 4.3, a ↦ a⊗u is a Jordan hom (via generators L_a of e^{tL_a} and (b)).
  Cor 4.4, U_{a⊗u} = U_a⊗id. Prop 4.5, p⊗q is a projection. Prop 4.7, operator commutation.
  Lemma 4.11, exchangeability tensors. These are REC 123–128 and 134 almost line by line; REC gets
  them from the effectus instead of the dynamical group.
- Thm 4.12 (A, B simple, nontrivial ⇒ AB special and UR). The key step: frames {p_i}, {q_j} give
  nm ≥ 4 pairwise exchangeable projections p_i⊗q_j. These have the same central cover, so every
  simple summand touched has rank ≥ 4, and rank ≥ 4 ⇒ special by the **classification**.
- Prop 4.14: if A has an exceptional ideal and B a nontrivial ideal, then no composite exists
  (a ↦ a⊗u₁ embeds A₀ in the special A₀B₁).

So BGW's proof **is** REC 135's proof in finite dimension. "Rank ≥ 4 ⇒ special" plays the role of
A–S *Geometry* Lemma 4.4 (REC 132), and "a frame of an exceptional simple summand" plays the role of
Shultz (REC 133). It gives nothing Shultz-free in infinite dimension.

**(c) Barnum–Wilce, "Local tomography and the Jordan structure of QT" (Found. Phys. 44, 2014).**
Finite-dimensional homogeneous self-dual models. Local tomography plus a factorisable self-dualising
inner product give Hanche-Olsen's condition, and then (a) applies. It inherits both obstructions of
(a).

**(d) van de Wetering.**
- JMP 60 (2019) (arXiv 1803.11139), Thm 3: finite dimension, local tomography (the linear V⊗V is
  the composite). The proof counts rank and dimension: rank r², dimension N² forces complex; for
  M₃(𝕆), no rank-9 EJA has dimension 729.
- Compositionality 2019 (arXiv 1801.05798), App. C: finite dimension, atomic effects, simple ⊗
  simple = simple, dimension count.
- The thesis cites BGW. Nothing infinite-dimensional about tensors was found.

**(e) Wilce, "Conjugates, filters and QM" (arXiv 1206.2897).** Finite dimension. It excludes 𝕆
by citing BGW/Hanche-Olsen (l. 1003: "one can form no satisfactory composite … exceptional").
No new tensor theorem.

**(f) Upmeier.** No tensor-product/exceptional theorem was found (the JB\*-triple work is unrelated).
Not pursued further.

## 2. Conclusion on "independent of Shultz"

No surveyed theorem covers infinite-dimensional JBW-algebras with REC's non-locally-tomographic
composite. The Hanche-Olsen line needs local tomography plus a qubit. The BGW/vdW line is
finite-dimensional and its core is the same rank argument REC already uses. **No literature theorem
can be cited as the single input for REC 135.** The smallest faithful inputs remain structural facts
about one JBW-algebra, not about composites.

## 3. A Shultz-free route (claimed, new; review before building)

What REC 135 needs from the exceptional corner W, a non-zero purely exceptional object:
- (i) some exchangeable family of n ≥ 2 projections summing to 1;
- then either A–S 4.4 (REC 132) on V_{W⊗W} (as printed), or the bounded-rank ideal (current Lean).

Shultz is used **only** for (i), in both routes. (ii)/`ExceptionalBoundedRank` is avoidable by
using REC 132 instead. Replace (i) by type theory:

- **(i′) `JBWNonAssocExchangeable`**: every JBW-algebra V that is not associative (∃ x y z,
  (xy)z ≠ x(yz)) has a non-zero central idempotent c such that c = p₁+…+p_n with n ≥ 2 and the
  p_i orthogonal and pairwise exchangeable by symmetries of V.
- Source: the type decomposition (H-O–S ch. 5, §5.3 for type I_n; halving in types II/III).
  **Exact numbers to locate.** For a type I_n summand with 2 ≤ n < ∞: n exchangeable abelian
  projections. For n infinite or types II/III: halve to get p ~ 1−p by a symmetry.
- A non-zero purely exceptional W is not associative. An associative JBW-algebra is C(X,ℝ) = the
  s.a. part of a commutative C\*-algebra, hence JW, which contradicts pure exceptionality. That is
  elementary, but it is a Gelfand step for associative JB-algebras.
- The central sub-corner cW is again a purely exceptional object, by REC's `corner_of_summand`.
  With the family n ≥ 2 on cW, `fam_tens` gives n² ≥ 4 in V_{cW⊗cW}. Then REC 132 makes it JW,
  REC 127 embeds cW, and that is a contradiction.
- Inputs of this route: `JBWExceptionalSummand` (from REC 52), (i′), and
  `AlfsenShultzFourExchangeable` (REC 132, verified A–S *Geometry* 4.4). **No Shultz 1979.** The
  tower in Rec136Hyps is not needed (n² ≥ 4 in one step), nor is the Albert algebra.

Comparison with the current `JBWExceptionalSummand + ExceptionalBoundedRank`:
- Neither is logically weaker. As Props these are theorems of different depth, not implications.
  (i′) is weaker than Shultz's (i): it asks for n ≥ 2 on some central cut, not n = 3 globally.
  But REC 132 is deep: Jacobson coordinatisation for n ≥ 4 plus a JW embedding, >3,000 Lean
  lines (Rec136Hyps docstring estimate). It is still Shultz-free.
- It is more elementary in *sourcing*: H-O–S + A–S only, both verified books, with no reliance on
  Shultz 1979 (checked only via secondary citations, rec-citations.md 2b).
- (ii) alternatives, if the author prefers to keep the tower: the bounded-rank ideal can come from a
  non-zero Jordan hom V → M₃(𝕆)_sa. That in turn comes from a factor representation plus A–S
  *Geometry* Thm 4.5 (a JBW-factor ≠ H₃(𝕆) is JW). This too is Shultz-free, but 4.5 contains 4.4.

## 4. Formalisability with REC's data (estimates, claimed)

- Hanche-Olsen 1985: irrelevant (hypotheses unavailable in REC).
- BGW 4.12: needs the finite-dimensional classification. Not usable in infinite dimension.
- (i′): needs comparison of projections, central covers, abelian projections, and halving in
  JBW-algebras. None of this is in the repo as far as Rec136Hyps shows. Estimate 1,500–3,000
  lines. It uses no tensor data, so REC's monoidal structure does not help. REC's data *do*
  supply what the tensor side needs (REC 124–134 are proved).
- REC 132: >3,000 lines (existing estimate). Nothing in REC's tensor data shortens it.
- Recommendation: if a Shultz-free REC 136 is wanted, name (i′) as a Prop next to the existing
  ones and prove `rec135` from `JBWExceptionalSummand ∧ (i′) ∧ AlfsenShultzFourExchangeable`
  (≈150 lines, reusing `fam_tens`/`corner_of_summand`/`rec130`-style embedding). Otherwise, keep
  the current `JBWBoundedObstruction`: it is the minimal single Prop for the Lean route, and no
  composite theorem in the literature beats it.

## Review (2026-09-26)

Spot-checked against BGW (arXiv 1606.09331, full text) and Barnum–Wilce (arXiv 1202.4513).
- **Hanche-Olsen 1985: STANDS** (as a quotation). The BW 2014 intro (p. 3, not "§1.4") states it for
  E a JB-algebra, E₂ = M₂(ℂ)_sa, with a Jordan product on the vector space E ⊗ E₂ satisfying
  (a⊗1)•(b⊗v) = (a•b)⊗v and (1⊗v)•(a⊗w) = a⊗(v•w). The conclusion is that E is the hermitian part
  of a C*-algebra. The "[JB-]" in the note's quote is the note's own insertion. The citation is
  LNM 1132 (1985) 223–229, doi 10.1007/BFb0074886, and matches BGW ref [29]. The two identities
  are exactly `rec125` / `rec125_right` (Monoidal.lean:1481/1515). The note's verdict (local
  tomography and a qubit are missing, so the theorem is not applicable) stands. The source itself
  was not seen.
- **BGW Prop 3.10** = [30] (CJM 1983) Thm 5.5 with operator commutation (iii). Confirmed.
- **BGW Def 4.1** is literally (a) a dynamical composite (Def 2.3), (b) (φ⊗ψ)† = φ†⊗ψ†, and (c)
  generation by pure tensors. The note's unpacking of (a) is plausible but was not checked
  line by line.
- **BGW Thm 4.12: STANDS.** Frames give pairwise equivalent projections p_i⊗q_j (Lemma 4.11). These
  have the same central cover (via [4] Lemma 3.9), so each touched simple summand has rank ≥ nm ≥ 4,
  hence special via the classification. Prop 4.7 is operator commutation and Prop 4.14 is the no-go
  for an exceptional ideal. All three confirmed.
- §3 (i′) route: not reviewed here. Its premise that "REC 132 is deep" is unchanged. Note that
  rec136-native.md (reviewed: STANDS mod G1/G2) needs neither (i′) nor REC 132.
